# Bibliography

Curated list of every datasheet, application note, standard, book, paper, reference project, report and dataset that **Super Spectral** needs on hand to go from research question to a validated wrist-worn analyzer. Treat it as the **acquisition list** and as the **traceability map**: anything marked must-have should be in the team's possession (or readily accessible) before its subsystem is designed, and every entry says *what it grounds* — a proposal section, an ADR, a validation metric, a roadmap phase or a firmware component — so that a reader of the [proposal](../proposal/01-super-spectral-proposal.md) or an [ADR](../adr/README.md) can walk back to the evidence, and a reader here can walk forward to the decision.

The method is inherited from the `swarm` project (same author): bibliography **before** implementation; index ([this directory](.)) separate from library ([`../datasheets/`](../datasheets/README.md), [`../app-notes/`](../app-notes/README.md), [`../standards/`](../standards/README.md), [`../books/`](../books/README.md), [`../papers/`](../papers/README.md), [`../reports/`](../reports/README.md), [`../reference-projects/`](../reference-projects/README.md)); one bulk acquisition pass ledgered in [`acquisition-status.md`](acquisition-status.md). The [documentation roadmap](../roadmap/documentation-roadmap.md) phases D1 (this list) → D3 (acquisition) → D4–D6 (study, ADRs, validation plan) drive it.

## How to use

| Priority | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| ★★★ | **Must-have / blocking.** You cannot complete the corresponding subsystem without this document. |
| ★★  | **Strongly recommended.** Builds depth, materially improves design quality. |
| ★    | **Useful background.** Fills gaps, gives alternative perspectives, or covers tangential topics. |

(The research session graded documents P0/P1/P2; those map to ★★★/★★/★ one-to-one, with a handful of deliberate promotions noted in each file's Disclosure.)

**Every row** has the shape `| # | Document | Identifier | Priority | Why |`, and the **Why cell names what the document grounds**: a proposal section ("§4 metric: sample-rate error"), an ADR ("ADR 0016 — VDD_SPI table"), a roadmap phase ("E2 eFuse read"), or a firmware component ("`twatch_bsp` AXP2101 rail order"). A Why cell that merely describes the document is a defect — fix it. Citations include enough metadata (document number, revision, ISBN-13, DOI, arXiv ID, repository + tag) that a librarian can locate each item unambiguously. Every file ends with **Acquisition links** (`| # | Access | Link |`) and a **Disclosure** section stating which entries were live-verified in the 2026-08-20 research session and which are model-recalled (low-confidence rows are also flagged inline "(verify)"). When a link rots, the identifier in the entry remains authoritative; DOIs always resolve at `https://doi.org/<DOI>`.

**Access vocabulary** (the `Access` column of every *Acquisition links* table). Base values: `free` (direct download, no gate) · `paid` (purchase; never committed) · `mirror` (third-party copy of a vendor document — record which revision it serves) · `free, reg.` (free behind a registration wall) · `free (GET)` (scriptable direct URL for the D3 `fetch()` pass) · `REPO` (a git repository, cloned not downloaded) · `PORTAL` (vendor page with the download behind a search or product-page click) · `HTML-ONLY` (living web page; capture by print-to-PDF with the date in the filename) · `request` (exists but is supplied only on request — vendor support, corresponding author) · `n/a` (acquisition target with no document yet: the row records a `TBD` to resolve in D4/E2, not a file to fetch). Composition rules: alternatives are joined with ` / ` in order of preference (`free / mirror` = vendor first, mirror as fallback; `paid / free` = the normative text is paid but a free equivalent covers our use); a parenthesised qualifier may follow a value (`free (sample)`, `REPO (browse only)`, `paid (verify)`); `**paid**` may be bolded to make the purchase list greppable. No other tokens — in particular no `—`: a row that has no document is `n/a`, a row whose document is unpublished is `request`.

**Citation address.** Cite an entry as `<file-number> #<entry>` — `01 #9` is the Knowles microphone datasheet, `05 #12` a paper. Thematic files 08–11 re-list entries that already live in a by-type file under that by-type address, and give *local* letter-prefixed numbers (`S1`, `R1`, `P1`, `A1`, `D1`) to rows whose by-type home was authored in parallel; 02 and 03 carry cross-maps from those local numbers to their home addresses. Numbering is **append-only**: never renumber, never reuse a number; retire an entry by striking its title and saying why. The research workflow's internal IDs (`DS-…`, `AN-…`, `PA-…`) are not used anywhere in the repository.

## Acquisition tips

- **Datasheets, schematics and app notes** are free. Download the PDF, **stamp the revision in the filename**, file it under the reference library: datasheets → [`../datasheets/<vendor>/<part>/`](../datasheets/README.md) (instruments under `instruments/<model>/`), app notes and programme-doc snapshots → [`../app-notes/<vendor>/`](../app-notes/README.md). Two things are specific to this project: (1) Espressif programme documentation **drifts with `stable`** — snapshot the **v6.0.2** rendering to PDF with the capture date in the filename, and re-snapshot only through the [upgrade procedure](../devenv/upgrade-procedure.md); (2) the gating acoustic numbers (Knowles acoustic table and response curve, parts of the ST7789 timing table) are **raster images** — `pdftotext` returns nothing, so `checked` in the [OCR ledger](../OCR/README.md) means a human read them and, for curves, digitised them (WebPlotDigitizer → CSV with a provenance block next to the PDF).
- **Standards** split cleanly: ITU-T / ITU-R / EBU / BIPM (GUM, VIM) / EUR-Lex / FDA / W3C / ETSI and ANSI S1.11-2004 (via law.resource.org) are **free**; IEC / ISO / current ANSI-ASA / DIN are **paid** (CHF 100–350 each; check institutional access). Buy only what changes a number — nothing paid is required for *relative* measurement. File under [`../standards/<body>/`](../standards/README.md); **paid texts are never committed**.
- **Papers**: an arXiv or author preprint is identical to the published version for our purposes; DOIs resolve via `doi.org`; ASHA journals (Švec & Granqvist, Titze & Winholtz, Patel et al.) are paywalled — institutional access, interlibrary loan, or e-mail the corresponding author (works ≈ half the time). File under [`../papers/by-topic/<topic>/`](../papers/README.md).
- **Technical reports and vendor pages** are free but often *living* pages (LilyGO wiki, Zephyr board doc, Espressif support policy): capture as print-to-PDF **with the date in the filename**, file under [`../reports/<agency>/`](../reports/README.md).
- **Reference projects** are cloned on demand into the gitignored [`../reference-projects/clones/`](../reference-projects/README.md); the catalogue's **License** column decides whether anything may be *read* only or *taken* (ADR 0004). Small self-contained files (the LilyGoLib factory firmware image, a single header) go under `artifacts/`.
- **Datasets** are fetched by manifest into the gitignored `datasets/corpora/` — never vendored; the licence ledger lives in [10](10-datasets-and-ground-truth.md) and [`../../datasets/README.md`](../../datasets/README.md).
- **Books**: prefer the latest edition; SpringerLink/Wiley titles are often in institutional subscriptions. Only **author- or publisher-sanctioned free copies** go under [`../books/`](../books/README.md); everything else stays physical and is tracked in [04](04-books.md) only.
- **Redistribution**: vendor PDFs whose terms permit it (Espressif, ITU, BIPM, EUR-Lex, distributor-hosted datasheets) are committed verbatim (`*.pdf binary` in `.gitattributes`); documents with unstated or restrictive terms (paid standards, some vendor schematics) are filed locally, registered by sha256 in [`../OCR/manifest.tsv`](../OCR/README.md), and represented in git by their `_notes.md`. Decide per document at filing time and say so in the `📥` blockquote.

**Coupling rule.** When a document lands in the library, add a `📥 Filed locally: <relative path>` blockquote to its bibliography entry (in the file's *Acquisition links* section) and update [`acquisition-status.md`](acquisition-status.md) so the index and the library never drift. Then run `python3 -m doc_ocr extract` ([`../../python-scripts/doc_ocr/`](../../python-scripts/README.md)) to generate the gitignored sidecar and register the document in the tracked manifest.

**Reading set with a time budget** (if only a day can be spent before code): Smith *SASP* peak-interpolation and real-FFT chapters ([04](04-books.md) #1, 2 h) · Heinzel 2002 and Harris 1978 ([05](05-papers.md), 2 h) · Švec & Granqvist 2010 ([05](05-papers.md) / [08](08-voice-metrology-on-the-wrist.md), 1 h) · McLeod & Wyvill 2005 and Boersma 1993 ([05](05-papers.md), 1 h) · Knowles SPM1423 datasheet with the digitised curve ([01](01-datasheets.md) #9, 30 min) · USB-Serial-JTAG console guide and the flash/PSRAM guide ([02](02-application-notes.md) #36, #29, 1 h) · ESP-IDF v6.0 migration index ([02](02-application-notes.md) #3, 30 min). [11](11-esp-idf-platform-and-toolchain.md) carries the longer environment reading set for E1.

## Disclosure

This bibliography was assembled in the 2026-08-20 research session (two multi-agent workflows, ≈ 26 agents) and consolidated by the author; each file's own **Disclosure** section says which entries were live-fetched or searched in that session and which are model-recalled. The highest-risk items — vendor deep links into IEC/ISO stores, edition years of paid standards, ISBNs of out-of-print books, exact application-note numbers — are flagged "(verify)" inline and must be checked before purchase or citation. The first acquisition pass (roadmap D3) ran on 2026-08-20: 44 documents filed and extracted (11 committed as redistributable, 33 kept local and registered by sha256 in [`../OCR/manifest.tsv`](../OCR/manifest.tsv)), 18 fetch failures and 2 quarantines ledgered in [`acquisition-status.md`](acquisition-status.md) with the reason legend; the `📥 Filed locally` blocks in each file are the authoritative per-entry state. Priorities are the research session's P0/P1/P2 grades mapped to ★★★/★★/★; where a file promoted an entry, its Disclosure says which and why.
